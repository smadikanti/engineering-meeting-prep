The meeting happened. Update the analysis so it stays true, before I forget the
parts that were not written down.

My notes from the meeting are below this prompt. They are rough and probably out of
order. Do not clean them up into a narrative. Use them to correct the record.

Produce this:

1. WHAT I GOT WRONG
   Every place the meeting contradicted the reconstruction or the diagram. What I
   believed, what is actually true, and who corrected it. Put this first, because
   it is the part I will otherwise skip.

2. QUESTIONS ANSWERED
   For each question I asked: the answer, who gave it, and whether it settled the
   matter or only narrowed it. Mark anything that is now a verified fact I can
   repeat elsewhere, with who said it and when. A paraphrase I half remember is not
   a verified fact, so if my notes are vague, mark it UNVERIFIED and I will check.

3. QUESTIONS I DID NOT GET TO
   Still open, and now with a better sense of who to ask and when.

4. NEW OPEN QUESTIONS
   Things the meeting surfaced that I did not know to ask about beforehand. These
   are usually the valuable ones, because they came from the history I do not have.

5. WHAT I COMMITTED TO
   Exactly what I said I would do, to whom, by when, in my words. If my notes are
   ambiguous about the date, flag it, because a missed commitment in month one
   costs more than the work itself.

6. WHAT CHANGED ABOUT THE DIRECTION
   Anything that moved between decided, assumed, and open. If the meeting hardened
   something that was previously open, note that the window for questioning it has
   closed.

7. WHAT TO WATCH
   Given what I heard, the two or three things most worth checking again in a month.
   Risks that were acknowledged but not addressed, dates that sounded optimistic,
   dependencies nobody owns.

8. THE UPDATED DIAGRAM
   Reissue the Mermaid diagram with the corrections applied. This version is the one
   I share if anyone asks how the system works, so it should be right rather than
   diplomatic.

Rules:
- Distinguish what someone said from what I concluded from what they said.
- Where my notes are thin, say the notes are thin rather than filling the gap with
  something plausible.
- Keep the tone factual. This is a record, not a performance review of the meeting
  or of me.
